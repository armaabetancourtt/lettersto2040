# Research Plan — Letters to 2040

## Research objective

Understand how people describe the parts of life they do and do not want technology to transform.

## Initial method

Start with qualitative interviews before designing a large survey.

### Interview format
- 20–35 minutes
- semi-structured
- conversational
- permission required before recording or quoting
- participants may remain anonymous

## Sample questions

1. What is something technology has made meaningfully better in your life?
2. What is something it has made worse?
3. What would you happily automate tomorrow?
4. What would you never want delegated to a machine?
5. When do you feel most human?
6. What are you optimistic about in the next 10–15 years?
7. What worries you?
8. What would you write in a letter to someone living in 2040?

## Sampling

Avoid interviewing only developers or people from the same age group.

Aim for variation across:
- age
- occupation
- technical literacy
- relationship to AI
- creative / technical / service work
- students / parents / professionals where feasible

This is not intended to be statistically representative research. Findings should be described as qualitative themes from the actual sample.

## Synthesis

After interviews:
1. transcribe or summarize,
2. code recurring themes,
3. preserve contradictory viewpoints,
4. identify memorable language,
5. separate researcher interpretation from participant statements,
6. avoid converting qualitative frequency into population claims.

## Ethics

- obtain consent
- allow quote withdrawal before publication
- do not expose sensitive personal information
- do not fabricate or “improve” quotes with AI
- clearly mark paraphrases
- credit participants according to their preference

## Output

Research should feed:
- narrative structure
- selected stories
- design decisions
- future survey questions
- project retrospective
